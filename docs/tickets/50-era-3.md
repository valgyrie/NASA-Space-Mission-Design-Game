# 50: Era 3, the Moon and the land from above (1969–1972)

**What to build:** the HQ Upgrade after Syncom 3 moves the Company into Era 3. Two Milestones arrive: the science package the Apollo 11 crew set up on the Moon, and Landsat 1. New orbits (the Moon's surface, Sun-synchronous), new Parts, Saturn V and Delta 900, Kennedy's LC-39 and Vandenberg, and a bigger Assembly Hall. Real History cards before and after each. Asked for by the team on 15 Nov 2026 ("continue the Eras until Artemis IV").

**Blocked by:** 18

**Status:** planned (15 Nov 2026); facts checked and numbers proposed 15 Nov 2026, ready for a coding agent once the open questions below are answered (or their proposed defaults accepted).

**Research:** [`docs/research/eras-3-5.md` § Era 3](../research/eras-3-5.md#era-3-19691972-the-moon-and-the-land-from-above) (facts, sources, every proposed number). Numbers below are **game estimates** unless they say "real".

**Design:**

- **Era 3 starts 1 January 1969.** HQ level 3. Thor-Delta and Delta D are already retired ("Retired: last flew in 1962 / 1965", real). Saturn V opens with the Era; **Delta 900 shows "Opens in 1972"** until the Game date reaches 23 July 1972, its real first flight (same rule as Atlas V in ticket 51).
- **Apollo 11 science package** (support Milestone). Client NASA. The player builds a **Science Package** like EASEP (real: a seismometer with its own solar panels, 34–36 W, two 15 W radioisotope heaters for the night, and a laser retroreflector; 77 kg together). It rides in the Lunar Module on a Saturn V that NASA pays for, so the Launch costs the Company nothing; Shipping goes to Kennedy. Destination: the **Moon's surface** at Tranquility Base (real: 0.674° N, 23.473° E). Data: **21 days** (real: NASA says "just three weeks" of data, 21 Jul–3 Aug and 19–25 Aug 1969; it stopped answering commands on 25 Aug, probably from overheating). Reward **$12M**, deadline 365 days. The Lunar Module takes at most **45 kg**. On the surface the Spacecraft does not need to spin: Pointing is not asked.
- **The lunar night.** A day on the Moon lasts about two Earth weeks, then about two weeks of night (real: NASA's 340-hour night). Apollo 11 landed in the lunar morning, with about 14 days of sunlight left. Proposed rule: sunlight share **0.67** over the 21-day Mission, and the battery must carry the Spacecraft through the longest dark stretch inside the Mission (**7 days**). With the proposed Parts, a Large Battery (720 Wh) just covers the Science Package and Dish Antenna (4 W × 168 h = 672 Wh); a Medium does not. See open question 1 for the alternative (sleep at night, like the real one).
- **Landsat 1.** Client NASA. A **Multispectral Scanner** (real: four bands, green, red and two near-infrared; built by Hughes) in a **Sun-synchronous orbit**: real **917 km, 99.2°**, crossing the equator at about 9:42 a.m. local time, seeing each place every 18 days. Launch on **Delta 900** from **Vandenberg** (rockets to polar orbits fly south over the Pacific, not over towns; real: NASA's preferred polar site). It points at Earth all the time: needs **Reaction Wheels** (real: four wheels held it to ±0.7° on three axes). Data: **365 days** (real design life: 1 year; it worked 5½ years, to 6 Jan 1978). Reward **$15M**, deadline 600 days. Alternative if a year is too long to wait: 180 days (open question 2).
- **New Parts** (game estimates, Mk 1; recipes in the research note):

  | Part | Slot | Mass | Numbers | Days to make | Factory |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Science Package | Payload | 12 kg | 2 W, 30 kB/day | 6 | Electronics Lab |
  | Multispectral Scanner | Payload | 10 kg | 4 W, 150 kB/day | 6 | Electronics Lab |
  | Reaction Wheels | Pointing | 6 kg | 3 W; steady aim | 5 | Metal Works |
  | Dish Antenna | Comms | 3 kg | 2 W, sends 400 kB/day; must point at Earth | 4 | Electronics Lab |
  | Fold-out Solar Panels | Power | 5 kg | 20 W in sunlight | 5 | Solar Fab |
  | Heavy Bus | Bus | 30 kg | carries 100 kg, 75 days to assemble | 15 | Metal Works |

  Checks: a Landsat-like design weighs 60 kg and uses 9 W against 12 W of panel power on average in low orbit (33% spare: yellow at Mk 1, green at Mk 2). An Apollo package weighs 41 kg (its 26 kg of Parts need the Large Bus). Anomalies: the Dish Antenna makes "Point the antenna" more likely without Reaction Wheels; Fold-out Panels make "Stuck solar panel" more likely than Solar Cells (real: one of Explorer 6's four paddles never fully opened, 1959).
- **Rockets and Launch Sites** (lift real; prices and reliability game estimates):
  - **Saturn V**: 111 m; S-IC (5 engines, 162 s), S-II (5 engines, 384 s), S-IVB (1 engine, 147 s), then "Off to the Moon" (the S-IVB's second burn, 347 s); real Apollo 11 timings. Lift: low Earth orbit 125,000 kg, to the Moon 45,000 kg. Whole launch only, paid by NASA for the Apollo Contract. Reliability 0.98.
  - **Delta 900**: about 32 m _(verify)_; Thor first stage with **nine** Castor II solid boosters, then the Delta second stage. Lift: Sun-synchronous 816 kg (real), low Earth orbit 1,680 kg. Rideshare $20,000/kg; whole launch $6M (no real price found). Reliability 0.97 (real: 2 flights, both worked, both from Vandenberg in 1972).
  - Launch Sites **Kennedy LC-39A** (28.608° N, 80.604° W) and **Vandenberg SLC-2W** (34.756° N, 120.622° W). Delta 900 really flew only from Vandenberg; offering it at the Cape would be alternate history, labelled like Wallops.
  - The Launch show plays each rocket's stages; for the Moon it adds "Off to the Moon" after the last burn and ends on the Moon.
- **Assembly Hall level 4** (Era 3) takes the Heavy Bus; its model grows (taller high bay). HQ level 3 and Hall level 4 Upgrades: proposed costShare 3 and 45 days (game estimate, continuing the table in `balance.ts`).
- **Space view and Test Run:** a package on the Moon's surface shows on the Moon; Sun-synchronous orbits show tilted just past the pole (99°).
- **Real History cards:** before Apollo 11 (Grumman's Lunar Module, the plan for science on the surface); after (the seismometer heard moonquakes for three weeks; the laser reflector is still used: the Moon moves away about 3.8 cm a year). Before Landsat (ERTS, first satellite to watch land); after (its TV cameras failed in two weeks, the scanner became the star; over 300,000 scenes). Facts and links in the research note; Learn more → NASA Science pages.
- **Like the real one:** Apollo 11 EASEP 77 kg, solar (seismometer); Landsat 1 about 953 kg _(sources differ: 816–1,800 kg)_, solar, worked to 6 Jan 1978.
- **Demo Mode:** an Era 3 save (Apollo 11 package accepted, a design ready).
- **Optional if time allows:** Propellant Plant (GDD §5: water → propellant, Era 3).

**Open questions for the team:**
1. Lunar night: keep the Power model (0.67 sunlight, the battery must last 7 dark days), or let a solar-only package **sleep** through the night and keep counting Data after sunrise, as the real one did (the Mission then takes about 36 days for 21 days of Data)?
2. Landsat's Data: 365 days (real design life, ~12 min at 1×) or 180 days (ten 18-day cycles, a game choice)?
3. Comms from the Moon: should antennas send less from the Moon (proposed: a fifth, so the Dish Antenna is needed), or keep one rate everywhere?
4. Lift: real rocket lift (Delta 900: 816 kg to Sun-synchronous) dwarfs the game's 60 kg Landsat, so mass stops mattering from Era 3 on. Keep real lift (as for Falcon 9 now; cost, site and Bus size then decide), or scale lift down for play?
5. Delta 900 waits until 1972 for its first real flight. Should the Game date jump to 1972 when Apollo 11 is done, or should the player wait (about 3 game years, ~36 min at 1×)?
6. Should the Computer slot open in Era 3 (the Basic Computer Part already exists), or wait for the Advanced Computer in Era 4?
