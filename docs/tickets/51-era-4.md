# 51: Era 4, telescopes and Moon maps (1990–2009)

**What to build:** the HQ Upgrade after Era 3 moves the Company into Era 4. Two Milestones: Hubble and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. Lunar orbit, the Space Shuttle and Atlas V, lithium batteries, and the biggest Assembly Hall. Asked for by the team on 15 Nov 2026.

**Research:** [`docs/research/eras-3-5.md` § Era 4](../research/eras-3-5.md#era-4-19902009-telescopes-and-moon-maps). Numbers below are **game estimates** unless they say "real".

**Design:**

- **Era 4 starts 1 January 1990.** HQ level 4. Saturn V and Delta 900 retire ("Retired: last flew in 1973 / 1972", real); the Space Shuttle and Atlas V 401 open (Atlas V first flew on 21 August 2002: it shows "Opens in 2002" until the Game date reaches it, and stays open in Era 5).
- **Hubble.** Client NASA. A **Space Telescope** Payload in Low Earth Orbit, carried up by the Space Shuttle (real: STS-31, Discovery, Pad 39B, 24 April 1990; deployed the next day about 611 km up; whole launch paid by NASA, so it costs the Company nothing). Orbit 611 km, tilt 28.5° _(tilt verify)_. Needs Reaction Wheels and a steady aim (no spin; real: four reaction wheels, gyroscopes and three Fine Guidance Sensors hold it to 0.007 arcsecond). Data: **365 days** (its first year; real design life 15 years with servicing). Reward **$20M**, deadline 600 days.
- **Hubble's "after" card** tells the real flaw: the main mirror was ground about 2 microns too flat at its edge (NASA's figures range from 2.2 to 4 microns; the card says "about 2 microns"), because the tool used to test it during polishing had been set up wrong. NASA announced it on 27 June 1990, two months after launch. Astronauts fixed it on the first servicing mission, STS-61, in December 1993, with a new camera (WFPC2) and correcting mirrors (COSTAR). It links to Ground Tests: test with tools you have checked, then test the whole thing. Do not say a full test would surely have caught it.
- **Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO).** Client NASA. A **Moon Mapper** (camera and laser altimeter, like LRO's LROC and LOLA) in a low **lunar orbit**: real **50 km, polar**. Launch on Atlas V 401 from Cape Canaveral **SLC-41** (real: 18 June 2009); the Spacecraft's own Main Engine brakes into lunar orbit (real: about 900 kg of LRO's 1,850 kg was fuel). Data: **365 days** (real: its one-year exploration mission from September 2009). Reward **$25M**, deadline 600 days. Its "after" card: LCROSS, launched with it, crashed its spent upper stage into Cabeus crater near the south pole on 9 October 2009 and found water (about 150 kg of vapour and ice in view).
- **Sunlight and Earth contact in lunar orbit:** real LRO was in full Sun two months twice a year and otherwise had eclipses of up to 48 minutes in each 113-minute lap; part of each lap is hidden from Earth behind the Moon. Proposed: sunlight share **0.75**; the antenna sends **0.75** of the time.

  Checks: a Hubble-like design weighs 168 kg; its 108 kg of Parts needs the Very Large Bus (Assembly Hall level 5). It uses 9 W against 12 W of panel power on average (33% spare). An LRO-like design weighs 94 kg and has only 7% spare power at Mk 1, so better panels or Low-power electronics matter. Its 150 kB/day needs a Ground Station if antennas send less from the Moon (ticket 50, open question 3).
- **Rockets** (lift real; prices and reliability game estimates):
  - **Space Shuttle**: 56 m stack; two solid boosters burn about 2 minutes (120 s) beside three main engines that run 8.5 minutes (510 s); then the orbiter opens its bay and lets the Spacecraft go. Lift 27,500 kg to low orbit (secondary source; NASA's reference gives no per-flight figure). Rideshare $15,000/kg; whole launch paid by NASA for Hubble. Reliability 0.99 (real: 135 flights, 1981–2011; Challenger was lost during launch in 1986). Launch Site Kennedy **LC-39B** (28.627° N, 80.621° W).
  - **Atlas V 401**: 58 m; RD-180 booster (255 s), Centaur upper stage (about 800 s, mission-dependent; real OA-7 timings). Lift 9,800 kg low orbit, 4,750 kg geostationary transfer (ULA); to the Moon about 2,700 kg (LRO with LCROSS) _(verify)_. Rideshare $12,000/kg. Reliability 0.99. Launch Site Cape Canaveral **SLC-41** (28.583° N, 80.583° W).
  - The Launch show plays their real stages.
- **Assembly Hall level 5** (Era 4) takes the Very Large Bus; its model grows again (a high bay with a big door). HQ level 4 and Hall level 5 Upgrades: proposed costShare 4 and 60 days.
- **Space view:** Spacecraft in lunar orbit circle the Moon (a 50 km polar orbit takes about 113 minutes).
- **Side story card:** Pegasus (first flight 5 April 1990), the first air-launched orbital rocket, dropped from NASA's B-52 at 43,198 ft with a 423-lb satellite; Orbital Sciences built it with private money. Private rockets begin. (Its maker's "first privately developed" claim is a company claim: say "built with private money".)
- **Like the real one:** Hubble about 10,800 kg (24,000 lb at launch), solar arrays, still working in 2026; LRO 1,850 kg with fuel, about 1,850 W of solar array and an 80 Ah lithium-ion battery, still working in 2026.
